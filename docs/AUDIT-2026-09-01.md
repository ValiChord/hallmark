## Z — Zome (Rust)

### Z1. CRITICAL — half the validation callbacks are unhandled

`crates/integrity/src/lib.rs:87`

```rust
_ => Ok(ValidateCallbackResult::Valid),
```

`FlatOp` has six variants (verified against `hdi-0.8.0/src/flat_op.rs:27-44`):
`CreateRecord`, `CreateEntry`, `AgentActivity`, `Link`, `Update`, `Delete`. The
zome handles four. **`CreateRecord` and `AgentActivity` fall into the catch-all
and are accepted unconditionally.**

Holochain validates the same data at two different authorities. The entry
authority runs these rules; the record authority is handed "valid, whatever".
So immutability, link rules and every membership rule are enforced on one side
only.

Attack sketch: commit a self-issued `MembershipProof` with
`issuer_membership_hash` pointing at a genuine OEM proof. The entry authority
rejects it (`validate.rs:66`, `:121`); the record authority stores it. Then
attest under it — `walk_membership_to_root` (`validate.rs:276-309`) only follows
`issuer_membership_hash` upward and never re-checks self-issuance, authorship,
depth or TTL at each hop, because those live in `validate_membership`, which the
forged proof never ran.

**Unverified:** whether a failed `StoreEntry` verdict raises a warrant that
withdraws the record network-wide in 0.7.0. The conductor crate is not on this
box. The exploit chain may be incomplete; the defect is not in doubt.

Fix: route `FlatOp::CreateRecord(OpRecord::CreateEntry{..})` into
`validate_create`, `CreateLink`/`DeleteLink` into `validate_link`,
`UpdateEntry`/`DeleteEntry` into the immutability rejection. Enumerate the
system variants you intend to permit, and make the residual `_` arm **Invalid**.
Same for `AgentActivity`.

### Z2. HIGH — anyone can permanently revoke any member

`crates/integrity/src/validate.rs:315-482`

`DuplicateDocument`, `ConflictingAssertions` and `DuplicateCertIssuance` check no
author at all — deliberately, so third parties can file objective evidence. But
nothing requires the cited evidence to have been authored under the membership
being revoked, nor within its validity window.

Once two conflicting attestations exist, those action hashes are public and
permanent. Any agent — no membership, no relationship to the parties — can cite
them against **every subsequent membership that shop is ever issued**. Entries
are immutable and revocations do not expire, so one historical slip becomes a
permanent, unappealable ejection executable by a stranger.

This is also the competition-law exposure: the ejection rule becomes a weapon.

Fix: in each evidence-based arm require
`membership.timestamp <= evidence.timestamp <= membership.expires_at`, and for
the attestation grounds require
`evidence.membership_proof_hash == revocation.membership_hash`. Both stay
deterministic.

### Z3. HIGH — a back-dated revocation destroys `historically_valid`

`crates/coordinator/src/verify.rs:290`; `validate.rs:315-482`

`validate_revocation` imposes **no timestamp constraint whatsoever** — it never
compares the revocation's action timestamp to the membership, the evidence, or
anything else. Contrast `validate_membership:125` and `validate_attestation:239`,
which both impose ordering.

An attacker with a quiescent chain commits a revocation stamped before the
target attestation. Every reader then reports `historically_valid: false`. That
directly falsifies the crate's own claim at `integrity/src/lib.rs:18-20` that
"an 8130-3 written while a shop was a member stays a real document".

Fix: reject a revocation predating the membership it revokes or the evidence it
cites. Separately, stop letting an author-controlled timestamp flip
`historically_valid`.

### Z4. HIGH — expiry binds only honest authors

`validate.rs:242`, `:128`, `:534`

Expiry is checked against `action.timestamp()`, which the author writes and
signs. A shop whose approval lapsed in January can idle its chain, then sign in
March with a December timestamp, and it validates. An OEM can mint new
memberships indefinitely after its own delegation expired by back-dating each
issuance.

The attacker's budget is the whole interval since their chain's last action, not
a clock-skew tolerance.

**There is no fully deterministic fix inside HDI.** This is a real limit of the
architecture. Options: require `Attestation.anchor` (the type exists at
`types.rs:112-116` and **is never validated anywhere**) and check a qualified
timestamp; or surface "action timestamp far from first-observed gossip time" in
the report; or at minimum stop claiming in docs and tests that back-dating is
prevented.

### Z5. HIGH — `DuplicateDocument` lacks the supersede exemption

`validate.rs:373-392` vs `:415-420`. `ConflictingAssertions` excuses a
predecessor chain; `DuplicateDocument` does not. An organisation issuing a
corrected form under the same `document_id`, correctly linked, hands anyone a
valid revocation of its own membership.

Related: `predecessor_of` detects only a *direct* link. For A1 ← A2 ← A3, a
revocation citing (A1, A3) passes the supersede check.

### Z6. HIGH — key rotation needs no handoff or acceptance

`validate.rs:90-104`, `:469-474`

`predecessor_membership_hash` requires no `KeyHandoff` signed by the old key and
no `KeyAcceptance`. Meanwhile the duplicate-grant detector *exempts* anything
claiming to be a rotation. So a corrupt OEM can grant the same certificate number
to two shops, tag the second as a rotation, and the `DuplicateCertIssuance`
ground is refused as "a key rotation". The old key never consented.

The handoff machinery is enforced only in `complete_key_rotation` in the
coordinator — which is to say, not enforced.

### Z7. MEDIUM — `verify_attestation`'s revocation lookup is fail-**open**

`verify.rs:72-82`, `:340-348`; contradicting the module's own claim at
`coordinator/src/lib.rs:3-4` ("fail-closed on missing data").

A node holding the link but not yet the revocation record gets `Ok(vec![])` and
reports `Clean` / `currently_trusted: true`. Errors on the counters path are
swallowed entirely by `if let Ok(..)`.

Fix: treat `Ok(None)` and a failed `to_app_option` as `lookup_failed`, and set
`RevocationCheck::Unknown` whenever it is set.

### Z8. MEDIUM — verification re-checks nothing integrity checked

`verify.rs:87-130`. `membership_chain_hashes` asserts only that each record
exists, deserializes, and terminates at a root. It never checks authorship,
self-issuance, parent-child agreement, depth or TTL at any hop. And
`verify_attestation` never compares `record.action().author()` against
`attestation.attester.agent_pubkey` — the single most important check in the
format, which integrity does perform (`validate.rs:174`).

Defence-in-depth on its own; the exploitation path when combined with Z1.

### Z9. MEDIUM — a routine key rotation untrusts the whole subtree

`verify.rs:256-307` treats any revocation of any ancestor as trust-ending
without inspecting `grounds`. `complete_key_rotation` always files a
`KeyRotated` revocation. So an OEM rotating its key marks every downstream shop
membership — and every attestation ever signed under them — `currently_trusted:
false`, permanently, with no remediation.

### Z10. MEDIUM — a counter-attestation names no target

`types.rs:212-218`. The entry is `{attester, agreement, discrepancy_notes}` with
no target hash; the binding lives only in the link. One counter can be linked to
unlimited attestations, and all of them validate. A broker can file one
`Disagree` and attach it to every attestation a competitor has published. There
is no delete path.

### Z11–Z14. LOW

- `validate.rs:583-595` — an `if` with a comment body and no statements. The
  condition is computed and discarded; the comment is also wrong. Delete it
  before anyone reads this file.
- `validate.rs:458-460` — unreachable check; `:453-457` already forced equality.
- `validate.rs:131-134` — depth overflow returns a host error, so the op looks
  like a validator fault rather than invalid data. Return `invalid(..)`.
- No length or cardinality bounds on any field. `organisation`, `notes`,
  `evidence_hashes` and the rest are unbounded up to Holochain's entry limit.
  Bloat rather than DoS, but the format is a wire contract.

### Z15. NOTE — `dna.yaml:10` ships `initial_members: []`

Safe: `genesis_self_check` rejects an empty root set. Flagged only because
placeholders get shipped.

### What the zome gets right

- **Validation is genuinely deterministic.** No `get`, no `get_links`, no clock,
  no randomness in `crates/integrity/`. 16 `must_get_valid_record` call sites and
  nothing else. This is the hard part and it is correct.
- **Authorship is checked against the claim in every entry type that has one** —
  attester, membership issuer, handoff by old key, acceptance by new key,
  counter-attester.
- **Trust anchors cannot be self-injected.** An agent who edits the roots lands
  on a different network, not a privileged position on this one.
- **Both chain walks are bounded and cycle-guarded** — capped depth *and* a seen
  set.
- **No panics, no `unwrap`, no `expect` in validation**, deliberately, with a
  comment explaining why `require_app_entry` converts errors to `Invalid`.
- **No exposed remote-call surface** — no `init`, no cap grants, no `call_remote`.
- **Revocation really is a new record.** Update, Delete and DeleteLink are all
  rejected.

## B — Web application

Verified against the live deployment, not only the source.

### B1. HIGH — a third-party script runs on every page of the live demo

`demo/server/middleware/grok-pwa.ts:99-109`, injected because
`vite.config.ts:179` sets `serverDir: "./server"`. Confirmed in the live
response body: the page loads `extensions.js` from `grok.com`.

It is the only external script on the page, pushed unconditionally, with no
integrity pin and no CSP to contain it. grok.com has full script execution on
the origin, and every visit is reported to them.

On a project about not trusting unverified claims, a reviewer opening devtools
finds it phoning home. Compounding it: the install query parameters return a
live page titled **"Add Grok App to your Home Screen"** — verified.

Fix: delete the middleware and the `serverDir` line. Title, favicon and
description already come from `__root.tsx`.

### B2. MEDIUM — `X-Forwarded-Host` is trusted and reflected

`grok-pwa.ts:33-37`. Verified live: sending a forged forwarded-host header
returns a manifest named after it. Not exploitable today (nothing caches those
responses); a cache-poisoning bug the day a CDN rule goes in front. No XSS — the
escaping and the slug regex both hold. Same deletion fixes it.

### B3. MEDIUM — no security headers at all

No CSP, no `X-Frame-Options`, no HSTS, no `X-Content-Type-Options`, no
`Referrer-Policy`. Confirmed on the live response. A tight CSP is nearly free
here — the page loads only its own assets plus Google Fonts — and is the one
control that would have contained B1.

### B4. HIGH (custodial) — a third party's OAuth secret is committed

`demo/src/lib/auth/preview.ts:19-21` carries a live `grok_preview` client secret
for `auth.grok.me`. Unreachable and tree-shaken out of the build, so not
exploitable against this site. The problem is that it is **xAI's** credential
sitting in this repo and its git history. Fine while private; a disclosure the
day it is not. Comes out with the dead auth module.

### B5. Dead scaffolding — roughly 2,500 lines

Evidence: import-graph analysis plus live HTTP probes returning 404.

| Module | Status |
|---|---|
| `src/lib/auth/*` (16 files) | Zero imports outside itself except a passthrough provider. `/api/auth/get-session` → 404 |
| `src/lib/db.ts` | Not imported anywhere. `DATABASE_URL` unset |
| `src/lib/app-data/*` | Zero imports. Inert on this host by allowlist |
| `src/lib/multiplayer/p2p.ts` | 570 lines of WebRTC polling `/api/rtc`, which **does not exist** — 404 |

Deleting these removes the committed secret, drops `better-auth`, `pg`,
`kysely`, `jose` and `pglite` from the tree, and removes a reviewer's "what is
this doing here?" moment.

### What the web app gets right

- **The production surface is genuinely minimal** — three static routes, zero
  server functions (`createServerFn` appears nowhere), no API handlers, no
  database, no writes.
- **The auth kill-switch is enforced server-side and fails closed.**
  `verify.server.ts:84-93` throws rather than fall back to a shared dev user.
  Inherited code that could easily have been fail-open.
- **Both `postMessage` handlers check origin** — including `event.source`, not
  just `event.origin`. The most commonly missed check in embedded code.
- **`npm audit`: 0 vulnerabilities**, dev dependencies included.
- **The `*.pdf` gitignore rule held.** No PDF, no `.env`, no key has ever been
  committed, and the home address appears nowhere in tracked files or history.

## D — Documents versus code

### D1. CONTRADICTED — expiry inheritance is not implemented

`SPEC.md:198` — "A delegated grant **MUST NOT** outlive the accreditation that
authorised it." `SPEC.md:348` lists it under **"Implemented and tested end to
end"**.

`validate.rs:128` checks the issuer had not expired *at issuance*. There is no
comparison of `proof.expires_at` against `issuer.expires_at` — verified by
grepping every `expires_at` in the file. `demo/src/lib/raf/validate.ts:109-138`
has the same gap.

It compounds: `validate.rs:273-275` skips ancestor expiry at attestation time
*because* it was supposedly checked at creation. So an attestation signed after
the OEM's approval lapsed verifies clean and reports `Active`.

This is the first question an aviation reviewer asks.

### D2. NOT IMPLEMENTED — the 13/14 split has no backend existence

`TECHNICAL-REFERENCE.md:108-110` states the two paths "are not interchangeable".

Verified: the `Attestation` entry (`types.rs:13-24`) carries **no field
distinguishing the paths**, and the strings `13a`, `14a`, `airworthiness` and
`return_to_service` appear **nowhere in `crates/`**. Both demo pages construct
the same entry through the same validation. The vocabulary check
(`validate.rs:198-220`) is one flat membership test with no partition by path,
document type or accreditation.

So: a repair station can assert `NEW`; a production holder can assert
`OVERHAULED`; the block-13 "no predecessor" rule is enforced only by the form
omitting the field; and a verifier cannot tell the two paths apart because the
record does not say.

The UI work is real and well-annotated. Scope the claim to it honestly, and
write down what making it real requires: a path discriminator on the binding,
vocabulary partitioned by path, and a signer requirement tied to production
versus maintenance accreditation.

### D3. CONTRADICTED — §8 confesses a limit that no longer exists

`TECHNICAL-REFERENCE.md:257` — "One conductor, two agents. Nothing exercises
gossip between separate nodes." But `network-gossip.mjs` runs two conductors in
CI on every push (`ci.yml:167-226`). The docs are hiding the best evidence the
project has. `README.md:187-192` and `HANDOVER.md:39` are stale the same way.

What genuinely remains untested: partitions, adversarial peers, separate
physical hosts.

### D4. CONTRADICTED — the conformance test does not do what it claims

`TECHNICAL-REFERENCE.md:240-242` — "If either moves, one of the two fails."

`conformance.test.ts` **never invokes the zome**. The verdicts are hand
transcribed from a run on 2026-09-01 and it compares four fields. Change the
Rust and nothing here fails. It is a TypeScript regression guard, not a
cross-implementation contract.

### D5. NOT IMPLEMENTED — SPEC §10 steps 3 and 5

Document-digest checking and qualified-timestamp checking are both listed as
implemented at `SPEC.md:350`. `verify_attestation` takes only an `ActionHash` —
there is no parameter through which a document could be presented — and the
`Anchor` field is never read. The TypeScript `Attestation` type has no `anchor`
field at all. §13 already admits this at line 356, contradicting line 350.

### D6. §13's "tested end to end" list is far wider than any test

The complete automated inventory: one happy path plus a `ConflictingAssertions`
revocation (single conductor), the same shape with an `Administrative`
revocation (two conductors), and two TypeScript assertions.

**Implemented and enforced but untested:** counter-attestation, delegation past
depth 1, the delegation matrix, depth limits, membership expiry, predecessor
chains, key rotation in its entirety, `DuplicateDocument`,
`DuplicateCertIssuance`, `KeyRotated`, immutability, `genesis_self_check`, all
eight link rules, and vocabulary rejection.

The **only** negative test in the repository is "non-root membership issuance
rejected" — and that one is short-circuited by the coordinator, so it does not
demonstrate the integrity rule it appears to.

### D7. Undisclosed limits

Present in the code, absent from §8:

- **`historically_valid` goes false when data has simply not arrived.**
  `verify.rs:164-166` and `:225-227` set it false for `MembershipCheck::NotFound`
  and `PredecessorCheck::Missing` — ordinary propagation states. A stranger who
  fetches an attestation before its membership gossips is told the document *was
  not properly authorised when signed*. That is a claim about 2026, not about
  their sync state. Deliberate fail-closed design, but it contradicts the plain
  meaning §10.1 gives the field.
- **`historically_valid` is judged against today's vocabulary.**
  `verify.rs:314-336`. Since the vocabulary is documented as provisional, the
  first revision retroactively invalidates every record using a removed term —
  the exact collapse of history into present that §10.1 calls the most important
  thing the format gets right.
- **Revocations are found only through links.** An entry whose link has not
  gossiped is invisible, and there is no `Unknown` for that case.
- **Neither implementation checks a signature.** Defensible in the zome
  (Holochain sys validation does it, and `verify.rs:11` says so); in the browser
  engine there are no signatures at all, and `verify.ts:210` hardcodes `true`.

### Claims that are verifiably TRUE — safe to state to a reviewer

- **`historically_valid` and `currently_trusted` are genuinely separate, and
  revocation flips exactly one.** `verify.rs:299-306` leaves `historically_valid`
  untouched on `RevokedAfterAssertion`. Tested three ways, including a revocation
  on one conductor changing another conductor's verdict on its own record.
- **Integrity validation is deterministic and uses only `must_get_*`.**
- **Revocation is verification-time only** — `validate_attestation` never touches
  a revocation.
- **Trust anchors are chosen at install.** The same `.happ` is installed twice
  under different roots in the tests.
- **Every evidence-bearing revocation ground is checked against fetched
  evidence, with no discretionary override anywhere in the file.** The two
  anti-false-positive carve-outs are real. This is the strongest
  competition-law-facing claim and it is fully backed.
- **CI proves more than the README says it does.**

## E — Efficiency and scaling

### Headline numbers

**One `verify_attestation`** = `3D + P + R + C + 3` network round trips, where D
is chain depth (capped at `max_delegation_depth + 1`), P a predecessor, R
revocations, C counters. At the shipped `maxDelegationDepth: 2`, a clean verify
is **13 round trips**; root-issued with no predecessor, **6**. It does **not**
grow with the number of attestations or the age of the network.

**One publish** costs `D + P + 4` `must_get_valid_record`, = 8 at depth 3.
**Constant in history length.** The predecessor check is deliberately
single-hop. The failure mode where a system gets slower forever is absent.

### E1. CRITICAL at scale — an unbounded hotspot nobody reads

`coordinator/src/lib.rs:138-143` writes an `AgentToAttestation` link on every
attestation, based at `agent:{pubkey}` — **one anchor per agent for life**. The
peers nearest that hash hold the entire set. A shop writing 200 forms a day
accumulates ~73,000 links on one anchor in year one, forever.

**Verified: nothing reads it.** `AgentToAttestation` appears exactly three times
in `crates/` — the enum, the create, and its validation arm. There is no
agent-to-attestation getter. It is written on every create, validated on every
node (one `must_get_valid_record` each), gossiped forever, and queried by nobody.

Fix: delete the link and its validation arm. Zero functional loss, removes a
permanent hotspot and an eighth of every publish's validation cost. If
agent-scoped queries are wanted later, shard the anchor by month.

### E2. CRITICAL at scale — N+1 where the batching API exists

`verify.rs:72-82`, `lib.rs:308-321`, `verify.rs:340-348` all do one `get_links`
then one `get` per link, in a loop. `hdk-0.7.0/src/hdk.rs:39,53` exposes
`get(Vec<GetInput>)` and `get_links(Vec<GetLinksInput>)`, reachable via
`HDK.with(..)`.

`get_attestations_for_serial` on a part with 40 certificates: **41 round trips
today, 2 with batching.** That is the query a relying party actually runs.

### E3. HIGH — the chain is walked twice per verify

`verify.rs:194` walks it via `chain_status`, which discards the result
(`:121-130`), then `:261` walks it again with identical arguments. **D+1 of the
13 round trips are provably redundant — 31% of a clean verify.** Five-line fix:
return the chain from `chain_status`.

### E4. HIGH — the persist store throws rather than degrades

`store.ts:23-44`. No `partialize`, no storage override, so the whole DHT is
`JSON.stringify`'d to `localStorage` synchronously on every action. Measured
1,320 bytes/record → the 5 MB budget is exhausted at **≈3,960 records**.
Zustand's `setItem` is not wrapped in try/catch on the sync path, so on quota
exhaustion React state advances, the write throws out of the click handler, and
memory and storage diverge silently until reload.

Fix: `partialize` the log out, cap `state.log` at ~200, catch quota.

### E5. HIGH — attestation creation is O(n²)

Measured: 500 → 20 ms, 2,000 → 208 ms, 5,000 → 1,527 ms. Two causes in
`engine.ts`: `nextTimestamp` (`:68-72`) scans all records for a max that is
always the last element, and `lookup` (`:75-81`) rebuilds a Map over all records
on every call, twice per `issueMembership`.

### E6. MEDIUM — 23 kB gzip of `zod` for a harness that no-ops

`preview-host-bridge.ts:8` imports zod to validate a five-field postMessage
envelope, and its component is mounted unconditionally in `__root.tsx:43`.
Measured at 85.6 kB raw / **23.8 kB gzip — 19% of the main chunk** — on a page
that otherwise ships ~100 kB gzip. Fix: dynamic-import it inside the effect
after checking that the page is actually embedded.

Total client JS is 574 kB raw / **172 kB gzip**, which is reasonable. Route
splitting works. Ten heavy dependencies including `recharts` and `pglite` are in
`package.json` and none reach the client.

### E7. MEDIUM — unmemoised work on every keystroke

`airworthiness.tsx:44-50` recomputes `eligible` — O(agents × records) — on every
render, and the component holds four text inputs. Also `workbench.tsx:629` and
`:812-813`. Measured 0.229 ms at 5,002 records on desktop, below the frame
budget today. Structural mistake, not a present problem.

### What is already efficient

- **Validation cost is O(1) in history length.** The failure mode that kills
  Holochain apps is absent.
- **Nothing large is stored on-DHT.** `Evidence` is a type, a digest and a
  locator — hash plus pointer. Entries measure ~1.3 kB.
- **No anchor tree is committed.** `.ensure()` is never called and paths are
  single-component, which avoids the worst version of E1.
- **Form state lives in leaf components.** Typing does not re-render the ledger
  or the agent list. The obvious mistake was avoided.
- **`validate.ts` is entirely Map-based** — the mirrored validation logic is the
  clean part.

### Could not verify

Whether Holochain caches `must_get_valid_record` within one validation call;
on-device phone timings (mobile figures are 4–6× desktop estimates); real DHT
latency, so zome costs are call counts, not milliseconds.

  A future HDI variant is a compile error rather than a silent `Valid`.

  outlive its issuer's accreditation.
- **Z13** — depth overflow returns `Invalid` rather than a host error.

Everything above compiles (`cargo check` clean).
